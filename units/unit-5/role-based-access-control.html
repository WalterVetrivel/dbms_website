Role Based Access Control

In a big organization, giving privileges to each person one by one is slow and error-prone. Role-based access control gives privileges to roles, such as Teacher or Accountant, and then gives roles to people. This page explains how it works in theory and in MySQL 8.4.

What is RBAC?

Definition

In role-based access control (RBAC), privileges (permissions) are given to roles, not directly to users. A role stands for a job, such as Teacher, Accountant or Student. Each user is assigned one or more roles and gets all the privileges of those roles.

Think of the uniform of a bus conductor. Whoever wears it may collect fares and issue tickets. When a new conductor joins, they get the uniform and the rights that come with it. Nobody writes a new list of rights for each person.

RBAC has three parts:

  • Users: the people or programs, such as Ravi or the results app.
  • Roles: named jobs, such as teacher.
  • Permissions: privileges on objects, such as UPDATE on marks.

Users are linked to roles, and roles are linked to permissions. A user can have many roles, and many users can share a role. RBAC is also called non-discretionary access control, because the administrator, not each owner, decides the roles.

Why roles help

A college has 200 teachers. Each needs the same 6 privileges.

  • Without roles (plain DAC): the DBA runs 200 × 6 = 1,200 grants. When a new table is added, 200 more grants are needed. It is easy to miss someone.
  • With roles: the DBA gives the 6 privileges to the teacher role once, then gives the role to 200 users. A new table needs one grant to the role.

Roles also make checks easy. To see what a teacher can do, look at one role, not 200 users.

Role hierarchies

Roles can be arranged in a hierarchy. A senior role inherits all the privileges of the roles below it, and adds its own.

  • Staff: read the timetable and the notice board.
  • Teacher inherits Staff, and can also enter marks.
  • HOD inherits Teacher, and can also approve the marks of the department.
  • Principal inherits HOD, and can also read all reports.
  • Accountant inherits Staff, and can also manage fees.

A hierarchy avoids repeating the same privileges in many roles. It also matches the way an organization is really arranged.

Separation of duties

Some pairs of jobs should never be done by the same person. If one person can both create a payment and approve it, they could pay themselves.

  • Static separation of duties: two roles are mutually exclusive. No user may ever hold both, such as fee_entry and fee_approver.
  • Dynamic separation of duties: a user may hold both roles, but may not use both in the same session.

RBAC makes these rules easy to state, because they are written about roles instead of about each person.

Worked example: roles in MySQL 8.4

We build the Staff, Teacher and HOD roles for a college database.

Step 1. Create the roles and give them privileges.

SQL (MySQL 8.4) 
CREATE ROLE 'staff', 'teacher', 'hod';

GRANT SELECT ON college.timetable TO 'staff';
GRANT SELECT ON college.notices   TO 'staff';

GRANT SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE ON college.marks TO 'teacher';

GRANT UPDATE (approved) ON college.marks TO 'hod';

Step 2. Build the hierarchy by granting roles to roles.

SQL (MySQL 8.4) 
GRANT 'staff'   TO 'teacher';   -- teacher inherits staff
GRANT 'teacher' TO 'hod';       -- hod inherits teacher (and staff)

Step 3. Give roles to users and make them active at login.

SQL (MySQL 8.4) 
CREATE USER 'ravi'@'%'  IDENTIFIED BY 'R@vi#2026';
CREATE USER 'latha'@'%' IDENTIFIED BY 'L@tha#2026';

GRANT 'teacher' TO 'ravi'@'%';
GRANT 'hod'     TO 'latha'@'%';

SET DEFAULT ROLE ALL TO 'ravi'@'%', 'latha'@'%';

Step 4. Check what Latha can do. USING expands the roles into their privileges.

SQL (MySQL 8.4) 
SHOW GRANTS FOR 'latha'@'%' USING 'hod';
-- SELECT on timetable and notices        (from staff)
-- SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE on marks        (from teacher)
-- UPDATE (approved) on marks             (from hod)
Common mistake

In MySQL, a granted role is not active until it is turned on. Without SET DEFAULT ROLE, Ravi logs in and gets “access denied”. Ravi can also turn it on for one session with SET ROLE 'teacher';. Check the active roles with SELECT CURRENT_ROLE();.

Worked example: when people change jobs

  1. A new teacher, Divya, joins. One grant is enough: GRANT 'teacher' TO 'divya'@'%';
  2. Ravi becomes HOD. Swap Ravi's role: REVOKE 'teacher' FROM 'ravi'@'%'; GRANT 'hod' TO 'ravi'@'%';
  3. The college adds an attendance table. One grant to the role reaches every teacher and HOD: GRANT SELECT, INSERT ON college.attendance TO 'teacher';
  4. Latha leaves the college. DROP USER 'latha'@'%'; removes the account and all its role links at once.

With plain DAC, each of these steps would need many separate grants or revokes, one per privilege.

Merits and demerits (advantages and disadvantages)

Merits (advantages)

  • Easy to manage: change a role once and every user with it is updated.
  • Least privilege: each user gets only what their job needs.
  • Matches the organization: roles follow real jobs and their ranks.
  • Easy to audit: checking a few roles is faster than checking thousands of users.
  • Supports separation of duties: conflicting roles can be kept apart.

Demerits (disadvantages)

  • Needs careful design: each job must be understood well to define the roles.
  • Role explosion: too many small, special roles become hard to manage.
  • Wrong roles are risky: a role with too many privileges gives them to everyone in it.
  • Less flexible for one-off needs: a single exception may need a new role.

In real life

College ERP

The ERP has roles such as Student, Faculty, HOD, Office and Admin. When a faculty member becomes HOD, the office only changes their role.

Bank branch

A cashier role can accept deposits, and a manager role approves large withdrawals. The two roles are kept apart so that one person cannot both make and approve a transfer.

Key points

  • RBAC gives privileges to roles and roles to users. A user can have many roles.
  • The three parts are users, roles and permissions.
  • In a role hierarchy, a senior role inherits the privileges of junior roles.
  • Separation of duties keeps conflicting roles, such as entry and approval, apart.
  • MySQL 8.4: CREATE ROLE, GRANT ... TO role, GRANT role TO user, then SET DEFAULT ROLE or SET ROLE.
  • RBAC is easier to manage at scale than DAC and more flexible than MAC.
