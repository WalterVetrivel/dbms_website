E-R Diagrams

An E-R diagram is the picture of an E-R model. This page gives a simple method to draw one from a problem statement. Then it works through three diagrams that exams often ask for: a university, a bank and a car insurance company.

The symbols at a glance

This site uses Chen notation, as in your notes. Each symbol is explained on the Entity-Relationship Model page.

Chen notation
SymbolStands for
RectangleEntity set
Double rectangleWeak entity set
DiamondRelationship set
Double diamondIdentifying relationship (links a weak entity set to its owner)
EllipseAttribute
Ellipse with the name underlinedKey attribute
Ellipse with a dashed underlinePartial key (discriminator) of a weak entity set
Double ellipseMultivalued attribute
Dashed ellipseDerived attribute
Ellipses joined to an ellipseComposite attribute and its parts
Single linePartial participation

How to draw an E-R diagram

Read the problem statement two or three times. Then follow these steps.

  1. Find the entity sets. Look for the important nouns: student, course, customer, car. Draw a rectangle for each.
  2. Find the attributes. List the facts kept about each entity set. Underline the key. Mark composite, multivalued and derived attributes.
  3. Find the relationship sets. Look for verbs that join two entity sets: takes, owns, borrows. Draw a diamond for each and join it to its entity sets.
  4. Place the relationship attributes. A fact that needs both sides, such as a grade or a date, goes on the diamond.
  5. Mark the mapping cardinality. For each relationship, ask “one or many?” from both sides. Write 1, N or M on the lines.
  6. Mark the participation. Ask “must every entity take part?” If yes, draw a double line.
  7. Find the weak entity sets. An entity set with no key of its own gets a double rectangle, a double diamond to its owner and a partial key.
Exam tip

Always write your assumptions under the diagram, for example “each car has one owner”. A problem statement never gives every rule. Examiners give marks for sensible, clearly stated assumptions.

Common mistakes
  • Drawing a relationship as an entity set. “Takes” is a verb, so it is a diamond.
  • Putting a foreign key such as DeptID on an entity set. In an E-R diagram the link is the relationship line. Foreign keys appear only after mapping to tables.
  • Putting a relationship attribute, such as Grade, on one of the entity sets.

Worked example 1: university

Problem: draw an E-R diagram for a university. Students take courses and get a grade. Instructors teach courses and advise students. Each course is offered in numbered sections.

  1. Entity sets: Student, Course and Instructor. Section is a weak entity set, because “Section 2” is unique only within one course.
  2. Attributes: Student(ID, Name (First, Last), Phone (multivalued), DOB, Age (derived)). Course(CourseID, Title, Credits). Instructor(InstID, IName, Salary). Section(SecNo as partial key, Semester).
  3. Relationship sets: Takes (Student, Course), Advisor (Instructor, Student), Teaches (Instructor, Course) and the identifying relationship Sec_of (Course, Section).
  4. Relationship attribute: Grade on Takes.
  5. Cardinality: Takes is M : N. Teaches is M : N. Advisor is 1 : N (one instructor advises many students). Sec_of is 1 : N.
  6. Participation: every student has an advisor (total). Every section belongs to a course (total).

Worked example 2: bank

Problem: draw an E-R diagram for a banking system with customers, accounts, loans and branches. Customers repay loans in installments (payments).

  1. Entity sets: Customer, Account, Loan and Branch. Payment is weak: payment number 1 exists for every loan.
  2. Attributes: Customer(CustID, CName, Address (Street, City), Phone (multivalued)). Account(AccNo, Balance). Loan(LoanNo, Amount). Branch(BranchName, City). Payment(PayNo as partial key, PayAmount).
  3. Relationship sets: Depositor (Customer, Account), Borrower (Customer, Loan), Acc_branch (Account, Branch), Loan_branch (Loan, Branch) and the identifying relationship Loan_pay (Loan, Payment).
  4. Cardinality: Depositor and Borrower are M : N, because joint accounts and joint loans are allowed. Each account and each loan belongs to one branch (N : 1).
  5. Participation: every account has a depositor, every loan has a borrower, and every account and loan has a branch. A customer may have no loan, so Customer is partial in Borrower.
Assumptions
  • A customer can hold many accounts and take many loans.
  • An account or a loan can be shared by more than one customer.
  • Each account and each loan is handled by exactly one branch.

Worked example 3: car insurance

Problem: draw an E-R diagram for a car insurance company. Customers own one or more cars each. Each car has zero or more recorded accidents. Each insurance policy covers one or more cars and has one or more premium payments. Each payment has a due date and the date it was received.

  1. Entity sets: Customer, Car, Accident and Policy. Premium_payment is weak: payment 3 exists for every policy.
  2. Attributes: Customer(CustID, CName, Address). Car(LicenseNo, Model, Year). Accident(ReportNo, AccDate, Place). Policy(PolicyID). Premium_payment(PayNo as partial key, DueDate, PaidOn).
  3. Relationship sets: Owns (Customer, Car), Participated (Car, Accident), Covers (Policy, Car) and the identifying relationship Pays (Policy, Premium_payment).
  4. Relationship attribute: DamageAmt on Participated: the damage to one car in one accident.
  5. Cardinality: Owns is 1 : N. Participated is M : N, because one accident can involve two cars. Covers is 1 : N.
  6. Participation: “one or more cars each” makes Customer total in Owns. “Zero or more accidents” makes Car partial in Participated. “Covers one or more cars” makes Policy total in Covers.
Assumptions
  • Each car has exactly one owner and is covered by at most one policy.
  • Every recorded accident involves at least one of the company's cars.
  • A premium payment is numbered within its policy: 1, 2, 3 and so on.

From tables back to a diagram

Some questions give tables and ask for the E-R diagram. Example:

Schema 
Employee(empno, name, office, age)
Books(isbn, title, authors, publisher)
Loan(empno, isbn, date)
  • Employee and Books each have their own key, so they are entity sets with the keys empno and isbn.
  • Loan holds only the two keys and a date. It is a relationship set Loan between Employee and Books, with date as its attribute.
  • An employee can borrow many books, and a book can be borrowed by many employees over time, so Loan is M : N.
  • A book can have many authors, so authors is a multivalued attribute (double ellipse).

In real life

College admissions

Applicant applies to Program is M : N, with Rank and Status on Applies. Each Program belongs to one Department (N : 1).

Hospital

Patient and Doctor are entity sets. Each test done on a patient is a weak entity set Test, numbered within the patient, with Performed_by linking it to a doctor.

Food delivery app

Customer places Order (1 : N), Order contains Item (M : N, with Quantity), and Restaurant prepares Order (1 : N).

Key points

  • Nouns become entity sets (rectangles); verbs become relationship sets (diamonds).
  • Underline the key of every strong entity set; give a weak entity set a dashed-underlined partial key.
  • A fact that needs both sides of a relationship is an attribute of the relationship.
  • Write 1, N or M on each line, and use a double line for total participation.
  • No foreign keys in an E-R diagram: the relationship line is the link.
  • Always state your assumptions under the diagram.
