Entity-Relationship Model

Before we build tables, we draw a picture of the data. The Entity-Relationship (E-R) model describes the things a college or a bank keeps data about, and how those things are related. This page explains every part of the model, and the explorer shows each symbol on a university diagram.

Where the E-R model fits

A database is designed in steps. The E-R model is used in the first three.

  1. Requirement analysis: talk to the users. Find out what data they need and what they do with it.
  2. Conceptual design: draw an E-R diagram. It is a simple, high-level picture of the data.
  3. Logical design: turn the E-R diagram into tables (relations). See ER-to-Relational Mapping, the third topic of this unit.
  4. Schema refinement: check the tables for redundancy and fix them by normalization.
  5. Physical design: choose indexes and file structures for speed.
  6. Application and security design: decide which users and programs may see or change which data.
Definition

The Entity-Relationship (E-R) model is a high-level, conceptual data model. It describes the data as entities, their attributes and the relationships among them.

The E-R model has three basic ideas: entity sets, relationship sets and attributes.

Entities and entity sets

Think of your college. Priya, a student with register number 7110231001, is one thing the college keeps data about. The course “Database Management System” is another.

Definitions
  • An entity is a real-world thing or object that can be told apart from all other objects. Example: the student Priya.
  • An entity set is a set of entities of the same type that share the same attributes. Example: all the students of the college.
  • An entity can be concrete, such as a person, a book or a car. It can also be abstract, such as a course, a loan or a holiday.
  • Entity sets do not have to be disjoint. A person can be in both the Student set and the Employee set, for example a research scholar who also teaches.
  • In an E-R diagram, an entity set is drawn as a rectangle with its name inside.

Elmasri and Navathe call an entity set an entity type. This site uses the Silberschatz term, entity set.

Attributes and their types

Definition

An attribute is a property that describes each entity of an entity set. Example: RollNo, Name and Phone of a student. The set of allowed values of an attribute is its domain.

In a diagram, an attribute is an ellipse joined to its entity set by a line. Attributes are of these types:

  • The types can combine. Address is composite and single-valued. Phone is simple and multivalued.
Exam tip

Age is the standard example of a derived attribute. If you store Age, it is wrong next year. Store DOB and calculate Age when you need it.

Relationship sets

Definitions
  • A relationship is an association among two or more entities. Example: Priya takes the DBMS course.
  • A relationship set is a set of relationships of the same type. Example: Takes, the set of all (student, course) pairs where the student takes the course.
  • A relationship set is drawn as a diamond, joined by lines to the entity sets that take part. We say these entity sets participate in it.
  • The degree of a relationship set is the number of entity sets in it. Most are binary (degree 2).
  • A relationship set can have its own descriptive attributes. Grade belongs to Takes: it is the grade of one student in one course.
  • The job an entity does in a relationship is its role. Roles matter when one entity set appears twice, as in a recursive relationship. Example: in Prerequisite, one course is the main course and another is its prerequisite.

Ternary relationships

A ternary relationship joins three entity sets. Suppose the price of a product depends on the product, the supplier and the customer. Then Price needs all three to identify it. We draw one diamond, Sells, joined to Product, Supplier and Customer, with Price as its attribute. Three separate binary relationships cannot store which supplier sold which product to which customer.

Mapping cardinality

Definition

Mapping cardinality (cardinality ratio) is the number of entities to which another entity can be associated through a relationship set. It is used mostly for binary relationship sets.

The four mapping cardinalities, for a relationship between A and B
CardinalityRuleExample
One to one (1 : 1)An entity in A is associated with at most one entity in B, and an entity in B with at most one entity in A.A department has one head; a person heads at most one department.
One to many (1 : N)An entity in A can be associated with any number of entities in B. An entity in B is associated with at most one entity in A.One customer places many orders; each order belongs to one customer.
Many to one (N : 1)An entity in A is associated with at most one entity in B. An entity in B can be associated with any number of entities in A.Many students belong to one department.
Many to many (M : N)An entity in A can be associated with any number of entities in B, and the other way round.A student takes many courses; a course has many students.

In Chen notation we write 1, N or M on the lines. Silberschatz draws an arrow towards the “one” side and a plain line towards the “many” side. Both mean the same thing.

Common mistake

Some notes define one to one as “associated with at least one entity”. The correct words are at most one. Cardinality gives the largest number of partners. Whether an entity must have a partner at all is a separate rule: participation.

Participation constraints

  • Total participation: every entity in the entity set takes part in at least one relationship. Drawn with a double line. Example: every loan has a borrower, so Loan has total participation in Borrower.
  • Partial participation: some entities may not take part. Drawn with a single line. Example: not every customer has a loan.

Cardinality and participation together describe a relationship fully. “A customer has zero or more loans” is partial participation with the “many” cardinality.

Weak entity sets

A college numbers the sections of each course as 1, 2 and 3. “Section 1” alone does not tell us which section we mean, because every course has a Section 1. We must say “Section 1 of course CS301”.

Definitions
  • A weak entity set is an entity set that does not have enough attributes to form a primary key. Its entities are identified through another entity set, the identifying (owner) entity set.
  • An entity set that has a primary key is a strong entity set.
  • The identifying relationship links the weak entity set to its owner.
  • The discriminator (partial key) is the set of attributes that tells apart the weak entities of one owner. Example: SecNo.
  • The primary key of a weak entity set is the primary key of the owner + the discriminator: (CourseID, SecNo).
  • A weak entity set always has total participation in its identifying relationship, and the relationship is many to one towards the owner.
  • Symbols: double rectangle for the weak entity set, double diamond for the identifying relationship, dashed underline for the discriminator.

Merits and demerits (advantages and disadvantages)

Merits (advantages)

  • Easy to understand: the diagram is a simple picture. Users who are not experts can read it and find mistakes.
  • Easy to convert to tables: each entity set and relationship set maps to relations in clear steps.
  • Good for communication: designers, programmers and users discuss one shared picture.
  • Independent of the DBMS: the same diagram can be built on MySQL, Oracle or any other system.

Demerits (disadvantages)

  • There is no single standard notation. Chen, crow's foot and UML diagrams all look different.
  • Some rules, such as “a student may take at most six courses”, cannot be shown in the diagram.
  • A diagram with hundreds of entity sets is hard to read.

In real life

Railway reservation

Passenger, Train and Station are entity sets. Books links a passenger to a train, with the attributes PNR and Seat. A ticket's passenger list is a weak entity set: passenger 1, 2 and 3 of one PNR.

Online shopping

Customer places Order is 1 : N. Order contains Product is M : N, with Quantity as an attribute of Contains.

EB bill

A consumer has a service number (key) and a multivalued attribute Phone. Each monthly bill is a weak entity set of the service connection.

Key points

  • The E-R model is a conceptual model built in the conceptual design step, before any table exists.
  • Entity set: rectangle. Attribute: ellipse. Relationship set: diamond.
  • Attribute types: simple or composite, single-valued or multivalued (double ellipse), stored or derived (dashed ellipse), and key (underlined).
  • Mapping cardinality: 1 : 1, 1 : N, N : 1 or M : N. It gives the largest number of partners.
  • Total participation (double line): every entity takes part. Partial (single line): some may not.
  • A weak entity set has no key of its own. Its key is the owner's key + its discriminator.
  • A relationship set can have descriptive attributes, such as Grade on Takes.
