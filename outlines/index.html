16-Mark Answer Outlines

Every 16-mark question from the question banks has a plan here: the headings to write, the diagrams and tables to draw, the examples to use, and the topic pages to study first.

Read this first: an outline is not an answer

These outlines only show you what to write and in what order. They are far too short to score marks on their own. If you copy the points as they are, you will fill less than one page and lose most of the marks.

In the university exam, a good 16-mark answer is at least 5 A4 pages, with neat diagrams, tables and examples. That is what earns 14 or 15 marks out of 16. Fill each point with your own explanation from the topic pages, your class notes and the textbook.

How to turn an outline into a 5-page answer

  1. Study before you plan. Open every topic page linked under the outline. Read its sections, try its widgets and learn its key points. Then read the same topic in your notes and textbook.
  2. Start with a short introduction. Give the definition in one or two exact sentences, then say why the idea matters. This takes about half a page.
  3. Write each heading as a heading. Underline it. Under it, write full sentences, not single words. Explain each point in two or three sentences, then give a small example.
  4. Draw every diagram the outline asks for. Use a pencil and a scale. Give it a title and label every part. Then explain the diagram in words below it. A diagram with no explanation earns few marks.
  5. Use tables for comparisons. When a question asks for a difference, an advantage or a type, a table with four to six rows is clear and easy for the examiner to mark.
  6. Work through an example. Use a small table of data or a short SQL query, and show each step. Examples prove that you understand the idea.
  7. End with a conclusion. Two or three sentences that sum up the answer. Do not add new points here.
  8. Watch the space plan. Each outline shows how much to write for each part. Together they add up to about 5 pages. Leave a line between parts so the answer is easy to read.

Time in the exam

A 16-mark answer usually needs about 30 to 35 minutes. Spend two minutes writing the headings in the margin first, so that you do not forget a part. Draw the diagrams as you reach them, not at the end.

For a question with parts a) and b), share the space by the marks. A 13-mark part gets about 4 pages and a 3-mark part about 1 page.
